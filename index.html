Decision support

Where Support May Be Needed

Screen counties using transparent criteria that combine economic disadvantage, college-going compared with similar counties, distance to a public four-year college, and income gaps. Criteria are counted, not scored. They point to places worth investigating with local partners, not to communities or students that are deficient.

How these findings could inform decisions

Geographic outreach priorities

Begin conversations in counties where college-going is below similar places, then confirm the pattern across several graduating classes with local schools.

College advising programs

Where the nearest public four-year campus is far away, ask about counselor caseloads, campus-visit access, virtual advising, and dual enrollment.

Scholarship & financial-aid help

Wide income gaps suggest examining FAFSA completion, aid awareness, and last-dollar scholarships for low-income graduates.

Grantmaking & program expansion

Pair this screen with a map of existing college-access programs (see the program file in the data guide) to avoid duplicating services.

Research partnerships

Origin-to-destination records linked to Pell status are not public. State data systems (MA DESE / E2C Hub, GA•AWARDS) and NSC can support approved research on them.

Interpretation

What the Data Can and Cannot Tell Us

Association is not causation

Counties with higher poverty may have lower college-going rates, but geography and income do not by themselves explain why. Many unmeasured factors differ between places.

County averages hide differences

A single county rate combines very different schools and neighborhoods. Large urban counties in particular contain wide internal gaps.

County poverty ≠ student income

County poverty describes all residents. It is not the income of any graduating student or family.

Pell receipt ≠ Pell eligibility

The share of students receiving Pell Grants at a college counts only students who enrolled and applied for aid. It cannot show eligible students who never enrolled.

Selectivity is one dimension

Admission selectivity is not the same as quality, fit, affordability, or completion. Two-year and open-access institutions are central to opportunity.

Missing is not zero

Hatched counties have no valid value: results were suppressed for privacy, not reported, or not collected. They are never treated as low.

States report differently

Massachusetts and Georgia use different low-income definitions, graduating classes, and matching rules. Comparisons with each state’s own average are more reliable than raw cross-state comparisons.

Public schools only

College-going rates cover public high school graduates. Private-school and home-schooled students are not included.
